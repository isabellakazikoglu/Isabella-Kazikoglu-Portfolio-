Financial Statement Analysis

Kimberly-Clark Financial Statement Analysis

An Excel-based analysis of Kimberly-Clark Corporation’s financial statements using information collected from SEC filings. The project includes ratio analysis, horizontal analysis, vertical analysis, and a review of selected accounting disclosures.

View Excel Workbook View Project Report

Project Overview

This project involved collecting financial information from Kimberly-Clark Corporation’s SEC filings and organizing the data into an Excel workbook for analysis.

The analysis compared the company’s financial performance and position across reporting periods using financial ratios, horizontal analysis, vertical analysis, and selected financial statement disclosures.

The purpose of the project was to apply financial accounting concepts to a real publicly traded company and use financial statement information to identify trends, strengths, risks, and changes in performance.

Project Objective

The objective was to evaluate Kimberly-Clark’s financial performance, liquidity, leverage, profitability, and cash flow using information reported in the company’s annual filings.

The project also required reviewing accounting disclosures to understand how the company recognizes revenue, records certain expenses, accounts for leases, and reports other significant financial information.

Analysis Performed

Ratio Analysis

Financial ratios were calculated to evaluate profitability, liquidity, leverage, and the company’s overall financial condition.

Horizontal Analysis

Financial statement balances were compared across reporting periods to identify increases, decreases, and significant changes over time.

Vertical Analysis

Income statement and balance sheet accounts were converted into percentages to evaluate their relationship to revenue and total assets.

Financial Statement Disclosures

Selected notes to the financial statements were reviewed to understand the accounting policies, estimates, and reporting decisions used by the company.

Cash Flow Analysis

Operating performance and free cash flow were reviewed to evaluate the company’s ability to generate cash from its business activities.

Trend Interpretation

Changes in financial results were evaluated together rather than in isolation to develop conclusions about the company’s performance and financial risks.

Selected Findings

Profitability Improved

Kimberly-Clark’s profit margin increased from approximately 9% in 2023 to approximately 14% in 2024, showing an improvement in the amount of net income generated from revenue.

High Financial Leverage

The company’s debt-to-assets ratio remained approximately 94% in both periods analyzed. This indicated that a large portion of the company’s assets was financed through liabilities.

Free Cash Flow Declined

Free cash flow decreased from approximately $1.188 billion to $885 million. Although the company continued generating positive free cash flow, the decline was an important trend to consider when evaluating financial flexibility.

Accounting Disclosures Reviewed

Revenue Recognition

The company’s revenue-recognition policy was reviewed to understand when sales are recorded and how revenue is presented in the financial statements.

Advertising Costs

The project reviewed when advertising costs are recognized as expenses and how those costs affect the company’s reported operating results.

Research and Development

Research and development costs were examined to understand how the company expenses these activities as they are incurred.

Cash and Cash Equivalents

The definition and classification of cash equivalents were reviewed to understand which highly liquid investments are included in reported cash balances.

Lease Accounting

Lease disclosures were reviewed to understand the company’s lease obligations and how lease-related assets and liabilities are reported.

Management Estimates

The project considered how accounting estimates and management judgment can affect reported balances and financial statement interpretation.

What I Learned

This project strengthened my ability to locate and interpret information from SEC filings and organize financial statement data in Excel.

I gained experience calculating financial ratios, comparing reporting periods, identifying significant trends, and connecting numerical results with information found in the notes to the financial statements.

The project also showed me why financial results should be evaluated together. An improvement in profitability does not automatically eliminate concerns involving leverage, cash flow, or other areas of financial risk.
